The island is drawn from nothing but code: no image files, no outside assets. Everything comes from a handful of hand-drawn sprites and four small palettes.

## Tiles and sprites

The map is 64 × 64 tiles, each tile 8 × 8 pixels. Buildings, trees and animals are sprites written as strings – one character per pixel, one letter per colour:

```text
'.....z..',
'..RRRzR.',
'.RrrrrrR',
'RrrrrrrR',
'RRRRRRRR',
'.vvvvvW.',
'.vyvdvW.',
'.vyvdvW.',
```

That is the house. Tall sprites like the lighthouse reach up into the tile above, and everything is drawn back to front so roofs overlap correctly.

## Soft coasts from hard tiles

The world is made of squares, but coastlines should not be. The renderer blends between the centres of neighbouring tiles (a bilinear "marching squares" field with a little noise), so convex corners round off and bays fill in. The sea darkens with the distance to the shore, dithered with a 4 × 4 Bayer matrix like on old consoles, and a foam line follows every coast.

## Four seasons

The date decides the palette. Autumn turns leaves orange, winter lays snow on meadows and rocks and leaves only the pines green.

## Animated, but only a little

The README image is an SVG with a few CSS keyframes: waves flip between two frames, windmill sails turn, boats bob, the lighthouse blinks and the newest tile is marked with blinking corners. With `prefers-reduced-motion` everything holds still.

## The same island in 3D

The website builds a 3D version from exactly the same data. Every tile becomes a block that rises out of the sea on the day it appeared; buildings are assembled from six shapes – box, cylinder, cone, pyramid, prism and icosahedron – and rendered with instancing, so the whole island is only a handful of draw calls. One persistent canvas stays alive behind every page, which is why the island never has to load twice.
