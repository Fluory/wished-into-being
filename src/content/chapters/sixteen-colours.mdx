Every wish becomes a **sprite**: sixteen by sixteen pixels in a palette of fifteen colours. Small enough to check
with a few lines of code, rich enough to draw a lighthouse, an owl or a very small library.

## The palette

Each colour has a letter. A dot is transparent. Gold, cream and orange **glow at night** – windows, flames, lanterns;
everything else is seen by moonlight.

## A drawing made of text

A sprite is sixteen lines of sixteen characters. That is the whole file format – it fits in an issue, in a JSON line
and in a code review.

<SpriteSource bottle="lantern" />

The code accepts a sprite when it has sixteen rows of sixteen characters from the palette, at least twenty coloured
pixels, at least two colours (an outline and a fill), and some transparent space. Things stand on the bottom rows: the
island is seen from above, the things on it from the front.

## When you only describe it

Most people will not draw. That is fine: Claude draws the sprite from the words in the wish. Before anything is
written, the routine renders its drawing large – with numbered rows and columns, and once more at island scale by
moonlight – and looks at it. If the cat looks like a potato, it draws again. Only a sprite that passes the checks
can be applied.

<Callout icon="✎">
  Want to draw it yourself? Put your sixteen lines into the <em>drawing</em> field of the wish form. The routine keeps
  it if it passes the checks, and fixes only what does not.
</Callout>

## Three ways to see it

- **In the README**, the island is a night picture: the still layers are an embedded PNG (a year-old island would
  otherwise weigh megabytes as vector paths), the moving ones – waves, twinkling stars, today’s marker – are SVG.
- **On this site**, every pixel of every sprite is a small cube. The figures turn to face you like paper theatre,
  pop up on the day their wish came true, and whatever is gold glows.
- **On its own**, every wish is also a small file, `world/sprites/w<id>.svg`, free to reuse under CC BY 4.0.
